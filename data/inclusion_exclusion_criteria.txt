INCLUSION / EXCLUSION CRITERIA
Review scope: Encodings in Quantum Natural Language Processing (QNLP)
Time window: 2021-2026

============================================================
INCLUSION CRITERIA
============================================================

IC1 - NLP task grounding
The method is applied to, or evaluated on, at least one natural language
processing task (e.g., classification, similarity, sequence labeling,
question answering, generation).

IC2 - Genuine quantum computation
The method is implemented and evaluated via at least a noiseless quantum
circuit simulation (exact statevector simulation or equivalent) of an
actual quantum model, or executed on real quantum hardware. Classical
algorithms that are merely "quantum-inspired" - e.g., classical
tensor-network models, classical approximations of quantum kernels, or
math framed in quantum-probability language with no circuit simulated or
executed - do NOT satisfy this criterion.

IC3 - Encoding scheme identified
The paper names or identifies at least the type of encoding used to map
text or text-derived features onto qubits, quantum states, or circuit
parameters (e.g., amplitude encoding, angle encoding, basis encoding,
DisCoCat / pregroup grammar, IQP feature map, data re-uploading, QRAM).
A named or typed encoding mention is sufficient; circuit diagrams or
full mathematical derivations are not required.

IC4 - Peer-reviewed or preprint venue
Published in a peer-reviewed journal/conference/workshop, or as a
preprint (e.g., arXiv) with sufficient methodological detail to assess
IC1-IC3.

IC5 - Language and access
Full text available in English (or a reliable English translation) and
accessible for full-text review (not abstract-only with no retrievable
text).

IC6 - Time window
Published between 2021 and 2026 inclusive.

============================================================
EXCLUSION CRITERIA
============================================================

EC1 - No NLP application
Quantum ML papers applied exclusively to non-text domains (images,
protein/genomic data, tabular/financial data, time series, vision-
language multimodal tasks) with no transferable text-encoding
discussion.

EC2 - Classical-only "encoding"
Papers using "encoding" purely in the classical sense (tokenization,
positional encoding, one-hot, byte-pair encoding) with no quantum
circuit or quantum state involved.

EC3 - Insufficient methodological detail
Abstracts, extended abstracts, posters, or short papers (<4 pages) that
name an encoding but provide no formal description, circuit
specification, or reproducible detail.

EC4 - Duplicate/superseded
Conference version superseded by an extended journal version - retain
only the more complete version; note the duplicate rather than
double-counting.

EC5 - Pure hardware/error-correction focus
Papers focused solely on quantum hardware, error mitigation, or
fault-tolerance benchmarking that happen to use an NLP dataset only as
a generic benchmarking payload, with no discussion of the encoding's
linguistic/semantic properties.

EC6 - Quantum-inspired, classical surrogate, or unverifiable simulation
The method is described as "quantum-inspired," "quantum-motivated," or
uses quantum-derived mathematical formalism (e.g., tensor-train
decompositions, density-matrix-style probability models), but no
genuine quantum circuit is simulated (not even noiselessly) or run on
hardware. This also covers results produced entirely by classical
surrogates that approximate or emulate quantum behavior, and papers
that claim "quantum simulation" without evidence (framework name,
circuit specification, library used - e.g., Qiskit, PennyLane, lambeq)
that an actual quantum circuit was simulated.

EC7 - Survey / review paper
The paper is primarily a survey, systematic review, literature review,
scientometric study, or overview of the QNLP / QML field rather than
an original research contribution presenting new methods, experiments,
or results. Tutorial and primer papers also fall under this criterion.

============================================================
NOTES FOR APPLICATION
============================================================
- A paper must satisfy ALL applicable Inclusion Criteria (IC1-IC6) to
  be included.
- A paper is excluded if it meets ANY of the Exclusion Criteria above,
  even if it otherwise satisfies the Inclusion Criteria.
- EC6 filters out "quantum-inspired" or quantum-flavored classical work
  that does not instantiate or execute an actual quantum circuit (IC2).
- EC7 applies to papers retained in the corpus for context but not
  counted as primary research contributions.
